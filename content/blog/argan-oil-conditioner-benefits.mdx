Argan oil conditioner benefits mostly come down to one thing: it coats the hair shaft with lightweight oils that smooth the surface, cut down on frizz and leave hair feeling softer and shinier. It doesn't repair split ends, it doesn't change your hair type, and it won't fix a scalp problem. Used regularly, as part of an ordinary wash routine, it does a genuinely useful job on dry, dull or slightly damaged hair, which is why it's one of the steadier sellers in any haircare cupboard.

This article explains what argan oil conditioner can and can't do, who tends to notice the biggest difference, and how to choose between a single bottle, a twin pack and a bigger bundle without overthinking it.

## Argan oil conditioner benefits: what it actually does

Argan oil comes from the kernels of the argan tree, traditionally grown in Morocco. In a conditioner, it's blended with other ingredients to help smooth the hair cuticle, the outer layer of each strand. When that layer lies flat, light reflects more evenly, so hair looks shinier. A flatter cuticle also means less friction between strands, which is what you notice as less frizz and fewer tangles when you run a brush through.

It's a cosmetic effect, not a structural one. The oil sits on the surface and in the outer layers of the hair rather than rebuilding anything inside the strand. That's true of every leave-on or rinse-out conditioner on the market, not just this one. If a product promises to repair damage from the inside, treat that claim with some scepticism.

## Who tends to notice the biggest difference

Dry, coarse or slightly damaged hair generally responds best to argan oil conditioner, because there's more roughness on the cuticle for it to smooth down. People who use heat styling tools often, or who live in hard water areas where hair can feel drier after washing, are usually the ones who notice a clearer before-and-after.

Fine or very oily hair can go the other way. Used too heavily, or applied too close to the roots, argan oil conditioner can leave fine hair looking flat rather than glossy. If that sounds like your hair, it's worth applying conditioner mainly from mid-length to ends and keeping the amount modest, rather than skipping it altogether.

## What it won't do, and when to see someone instead

Argan oil conditioner is not a treatment for hair loss, thinning or a scalp condition. If you're losing more hair than usual, or your scalp is persistently itchy, flaky or sore, that's a conversation for a pharmacist or GP, not something to solve with a different conditioner. The same goes for sudden changes in hair texture or breakage: worth getting checked rather than guessed at.

It also won't undo damage that's already happened. Split ends don't reattach, and badly over-processed hair, from heavy bleaching for example, needs a trim and a change in routine more than it needs a richer conditioner. What argan oil conditioner is good at is making the hair you currently have look and feel better day to day, and making it a bit more manageable to brush, style and dry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does argan oil conditioner make hair grow faster?

No. There's no evidence that argan oil conditioner affects hair growth. What it does is smooth the hair you already have, which can make it look fuller and healthier, but it doesn't change the rate at which hair grows from the scalp.

### Can I use argan oil conditioner every day?

You can, but whether you should depends on your hair type. Fine or oily hair may feel weighed down with daily use, while dry or coarse hair often handles it well. If hair starts looking flat or greasy sooner than usual, try conditioning every other wash instead.

### Is argan oil conditioner suitable for coloured hair?

Generally yes, since it works on the outside of the hair shaft rather than altering colour chemically. If you've had a specific colour treatment recently, it's worth checking with your hairdresser or the product you used for colour-specific aftercare advice, since some treatments have their own recommended routine.

### What's the difference between argan oil shampoo and conditioner?

The shampoo cleanses the hair and scalp, removing dirt, oil and product build-up, while the conditioner is applied afterwards to smooth the cuticle and add softness. They're designed to be used together rather than as alternatives to each other.

### Will argan oil conditioner fix frizzy hair completely?

It will reduce frizz by smoothing the hair surface, but it won't eliminate it entirely, especially in humid weather or with naturally curly or coarse hair. For a fuller routine on managing frizz day to day, see [this guide on stopping frizzy hair](/blog/how-to-stop-frizzy-hair).
