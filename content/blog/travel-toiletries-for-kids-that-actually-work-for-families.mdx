Good travel toiletries for kids are small, spill-proof and familiar. The easiest way to avoid a holiday bathroom meltdown is to pack scaled-down versions of what your child already uses at home: a compact toothpaste tube in a flavour they'll actually accept, and a solid shampoo bar that doesn't leak, doesn't count as a liquid in the usual sense, and survives a shared bathroom with siblings fighting over the sink.

This matters more than it sounds. A child who refuses mint toothpaste at home will refuse it twice as hard in an unfamiliar bathroom at 9pm after a long travel day. The toiletries you pack should make the trip easier, not add one more thing to negotiate.

## What good travel toiletries for kids have in common

The toiletries that work well for family trips share a few traits:

- **Small size.** A 60g toothpaste tube or a 60g shampoo bar takes up almost no space in a packing cube, which matters when you're also fitting in swimwear, a spare outfit for the plane and whatever toy cannot be left behind.
- **No spill risk.** Liquids in half-used bottles are the classic suitcase disaster. A solid bar simply can't leak over the pyjamas.
- **Familiar to the child.** Holiday is not the time to introduce a brand new flavour or texture for the first time. If you're going to try something new, do it at home first.
- **Easy to share fairly between children.** If you're travelling with more than one child, it helps to have enough of each flavour or scent that nobody feels short-changed.

Airline hand luggage rules on liquids vary by airport and do change from time to time, so always check the current rules for the airport you're flying from before you pack. This is one of the main reasons solid toiletries have become popular for family travel: a shampoo bar in a tin isn't subject to the same liquid limits as a bottle, which takes one source of departure-day stress off the table. ## Packing for more than one child without overspending

Family travel toiletries add up fast once you multiply by two or three children, especially if everyone insists on a different toothpaste flavour or a different bar scent. A few practical ways to keep the cost down:

- Buy the bigger bundle rather than several small ones. Bigger packs of toothpaste and shampoo bars cost less per item than buying singles each time, so check the live price on Amazon before assuming the smaller pack is cheaper.
- Keep a dedicated travel toiletries bag packed between trips, topped up as needed, rather than raiding the bathroom cabinet the night before you leave.
- Buy once for the whole school year of trips rather than restocking before every single one. A 6 pack or 12 pack of toothpaste, split between home use and the travel bag, usually works out better value than buying single tubes each time you pack.

## Common packing mistakes worth avoiding

A few things come up again and again when families pack toiletries for a trip:

- Packing a brand new flavour or product for the first time on the trip itself. Test it at home first, even for a few days.
- Assuming every airport has the same liquid rules. They don't, and the rules change, so check before you pack rather than finding out at the gate.
- Packing one tube of toothpaste for several children and running out halfway through the trip. A multi-pack solves this without you needing to do the maths on the day.
- Forgetting that a solid bar needs to dry out between uses to last properly. A small tin with holes, or simply letting it air dry on the side rather than sitting in a puddle, makes it last the whole trip.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I take toothpaste in hand luggage on a plane?

Usually yes, but liquid and gel toothpaste counts towards your airport's liquids allowance, and the exact limits vary by airport and do change. Check the current rules for your departure airport before you pack, and consider a compact tube to keep it simple.

### Are shampoo bars actually practical for a family holiday?

Yes. A solid 2-in-1 bar does the job of a shampoo bottle and a conditioner bottle without the leak risk of a half-used liquid bottle, and it takes up very little space in a packing cube or wash bag.

### My toddler refuses mint toothpaste, what should I pack for travel?

Pack whichever flavour your child already accepts at home rather than introducing a new one on the trip. Fruity options such as Watermelon Fresh or Peachy Clean, or sweeter ones like Yummy Gummy, tend to go down better with toddlers who won't tolerate mint.

### How much toothpaste should my child use on holiday?

The same amount as at home, by age: a smear for children under 3, a pea-sized amount for children aged 3 to 6, both with fluoride toothpaste appropriate to their age, and the full NHS-recommended amount for adults. Check the fluoride level on the pack to confirm it suits your child's age.

### Do I need separate toiletries for each child on a trip?

Not necessarily, but it helps. A shared tube of toothpaste or a shared shampoo bar can run out faster than expected, especially on longer trips, so a multi-pack with enough for everyone tends to work out simpler and better value than relying on one tube for the whole family.
