Here's how to use a shampoo bar: soak your hair thoroughly, wet the bar, then either rub it between your hands to make a lather or glide it down your hair a few times. Massage the lather into your scalp with your fingertips, rinse thoroughly and condition if your hair needs it. Between washes, keep the bar on a draining dish so it dries out and lasts longer.

That's the short version. If you've tried a bar before and found your hair felt heavy, waxy or tangled, don't give up on them yet. This guide covers how to lather and rinse, how to adapt the method for curly, long or fine hair, how to store your bar, and what to do if things don't feel quite right.

## What is a shampoo bar?

A shampoo bar is shampoo in solid form. Liquid shampoo is mostly water; a bar packs its cleansing ingredients into a compact block with no plastic bottle. Some bars are shampoo only, while 2-in-1 bars cleanse and condition in one step.

Broadly, there are two types, and it's useful to know which you have:

- **Soap-based bars** are made the traditional way, from oils and fats turned into soap. On an ingredients list you'll see names like sodium olivate or sodium cocoate. They can react with hard water and leave a residue on hair.
- **Syndet bars** (short for "synthetic detergent") use similar cleansers to liquid shampoo, such as sodium cocoyl isethionate. They generally behave more like the shampoo you're used to.

Neither type is better for everyone, but if you live in a hard water area and your hair feels coated after using a bar, the type is often why.

## How to use a shampoo bar, step by step

1. **Soak your hair.** Get it completely wet, right through to the scalp. Bars need more water than liquid shampoo to lather well.
2. **Wet the bar.** Hold it under the water for a few seconds.
3. **Make a lather.** Either rub the bar between your wet hands until you have a good lather and apply it like normal shampoo, or glide the bar directly over your hair from the roots downwards, three to five strokes per section.
4. **Work it into your scalp.** Massage with your fingertips (not your nails) for about a minute, adding a splash of water if the lather feels thick or sticky.
5. **Rinse, then rinse again.** Rinse for longer than you would with liquid shampoo. Leftover residue is the most common reason hair feels heavy or waxy after a bar.
6. **Condition if you need to.** A 2-in-1 bar cleanses and conditions in one step, which suits many hair types. If your ends are very dry or your hair is long, add conditioner or a leave-in to the lengths.
7. **Put the bar away dry.** Shake off excess water and place it on a draining dish, out of the shower's spray.

<Callout type="tip" title="Glide, don't circle">If you rub a bar directly on your hair, always move it in the direction your hair grows, from root to tip. Rubbing in circles, especially on long or curly hair, is a fast track to tangles.</Callout>

## Adapting the method for your hair type

The basic steps work for everyone, but a few tweaks help.

### Long hair

Split your hair into two or three sections and glide the bar down each one. Focus the lather on your scalp, then let it run through the lengths as you rinse.

### Curly, coily and afro hair

Lather in your hands first rather than rubbing the bar on your hair, then work it into the scalp in sections. Curly and coily hair tends to be drier, so follow a 2-in-1 with a leave-in conditioner on the lengths if they feel thirsty.

### Fine or oily hair

A few strokes over the scalp area is usually enough. Rinse especially thoroughly, because residue shows up faster on fine hair.

### Short hair

Lathering in your hands and washing as you would with liquid shampoo is quickest. Short hair tends to take to bars very easily.

## How to store a shampoo bar so it lasts

A bar that sits in a puddle turns soft and mushy and wears away much faster. Keep it in good shape with these habits:

- **Use a draining soap dish or rack** so water runs away from the bar.
- **Keep it out of the direct spray** of the shower.
- **Let it dry fully between uses.** A dry bar lasts noticeably longer than a damp one.
- **Cut it in half.** Use one piece at a time and keep the other dry in a cupboard.
- **Travel tins are handy,** but let the bar dry before you close the lid, or open it to air when you arrive.

How long a bar lasts depends on your hair length, how often you wash and how dry you keep it, so it's hard to give a single figure. Good storage makes the biggest difference.

## Troubleshooting: waxy, heavy or dull hair

Switching from liquid shampoo can take a few washes to get right. You may read that hair needs weeks to "detox" when you change. There's little evidence for that. A heavy or waxy feel is more often down to technique or water, and these fixes are worth trying first:

| Problem | Likely cause | What to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waxy or coated feel | Not rinsing enough, or a soap-based bar in hard water | Rinse for longer; consider a syndet bar |
| Weak lather | Hair or bar not wet enough | Add more water to your hair and the bar |
| Tangles | Rubbing the bar in circles | Glide it from root to tip, or lather in your hands |
| Greasy roots | Not enough lather at the scalp, or residue | Massage the scalp for longer and rinse well |
| Dry ends | A 2-in-1 isn't enough conditioning for your hair | Add conditioner or a leave-in to the lengths |
| Soft, mushy bar | Sitting in water between uses | Use a draining dish and keep it out of the spray |

Some people in hard water areas also like an occasional rinse with a little apple cider vinegar diluted in plenty of water. If you try it, keep it well away from your eyes and don't use it on broken or irritated skin.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need conditioner after a shampoo bar?

Not always. A 2-in-1 bar cleanses and conditions in one step, which is enough for many people, especially with short or normal hair. If your hair is long, curly or very dry, add conditioner or a leave-in to your mid-lengths and ends.

### Why does my hair feel waxy after using a shampoo bar?

The usual causes are not rinsing for long enough, or using a soap-based bar in hard water, which can leave a residue. Rinse for longer than you would with liquid shampoo and make sure you're working up a proper lather. If it keeps happening, a syndet bar may suit your water better.

### Can you take a shampoo bar in hand luggage?

Solid shampoo bars are generally not counted as liquids at airport security, which makes them handy for hand luggage. Rules vary between airports and have been changing, so always check your departure airport's current guidance before you travel.

### Should I rub the shampoo bar directly on my hair?

You can, as long as you glide it from root to tip rather than rubbing in circles. If your hair is long, curly or tangles easily, lathering the bar between your hands first is gentler. Either way, focus the lather on your scalp.

### How do I stop my shampoo bar going soft?

Keep it on a draining dish out of the shower spray and let it dry fully between uses. Cutting the bar in half and using one piece at a time also helps, because the spare stays completely dry until you need it.
