The best shampoo for a dry scalp is a mild, moisturising one that cleans without stripping the skin's natural oils. Look for a gentle formula with conditioning ingredients such as glycerin, panthenol, aloe vera or argan oil, and skip anything that leaves your scalp feeling squeaky or tight. Wash with lukewarm water, massage rather than scrub, and keep conditioner on your lengths.

One check comes first: flakes don't always mean a dry scalp. If your flakes are larger, greasy or yellowish, it's more likely to be dandruff, which usually needs an anti-dandruff shampoo instead. Below, we'll help you tell the two apart, then cover what causes a dry scalp, what to look for on a shampoo label, a wash routine that helps, and when to see a pharmacist or GP.

## Quick answer: what shampoo is good for a dry scalp?

If your scalp feels tight, itchy or shows small, fine white flakes, these are the things that matter most:

- **A gentle, moisturising shampoo.** Words like "gentle", "mild", "moisturising" or "for dry hair" on the label are good signposts.
- **Conditioning ingredients.** Glycerin, panthenol (provitamin B5), aloe vera and oils such as argan help the skin and hair feel comfortable after washing.
- **Lukewarm water and less scrubbing.** Hot water and fingernails make a dry scalp worse whatever shampoo you use.
- **A conditioner for your lengths.** A dry scalp often comes with dry ends, so pair your shampoo with a conditioner and keep it off the roots.

## Dry scalp or dandruff? How to tell

They look alike, but they have different causes and need different shampoos.

| | Dry scalp | Dandruff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How the scalp feels | Tight, itchy, sometimes a little sore | Itchy, often oily |
| Flakes | Small, fine and white | Larger, can look greasy or yellowish |
| Hair | Often dry too | Can be oily at the roots |
| When it's worse | Winter, central heating, hot showers | Can come and go at any time of year |
| What usually helps | A gentle, moisturising shampoo and lukewarm water | An anti-dandruff shampoo |

A dry scalp is simply skin that's short on moisture, much like dry skin on your hands in winter. Dandruff is different: it's a very common condition that isn't contagious or harmful, often comes with an oily scalp, and isn't caused by poor hygiene.

For dandruff, the NHS recommends an anti-dandruff shampoo with an active ingredient such as selenium sulphide, ketoconazole, salicylic acid or coal tar, used for a month to see if it helps. A pharmacist can explain how to use them. A moisturising shampoo won't treat dandruff, and a strong anti-dandruff shampoo isn't what a dry scalp needs, so it's worth getting this step right.

## What causes a dry scalp?

Your scalp makes a natural oil called sebum that keeps the skin and hair comfortable. A dry scalp usually means that oil is being stripped away faster than it's replaced, or the air around you is pulling moisture out. Common causes include:

- **Cold weather and central heating.** UK winters and dry indoor air are the classic trigger, which is why many people only notice a dry scalp from October onwards.
- **Hot showers.** Very hot water strips oils from the skin on your scalp just as it does on the rest of your body.
- **Harsh or frequent washing.** A strong cleanser used every day can leave the scalp tight and itchy.
- **Hard water.** Many parts of the UK have hard water, which can leave mineral deposits that make the scalp and hair feel rough.
- **Product build-up.** Dry shampoo, sprays and heavy styling products can irritate the scalp if they're not rinsed out properly.
- **Heat styling close to the roots.** A hairdryer on its hottest setting held close to the scalp dries the skin out.

If your scalp is very itchy, red, sore or swollen, or the flaking spreads to your face or body, see a GP. Those signs can point to a skin condition that needs a different treatment.

## What to look for in a shampoo for a dry scalp

A shampoo's first job is to clean the scalp. For a dry scalp, you want one that does that gently and leaves the skin feeling comfortable, not squeaky.

### Gentle cleansing

Look for products described as gentle, mild or suitable for frequent use. Softer cleansers remove sweat, oil and product build-up without stripping the scalp. If strong shampoos leave your scalp tight, a sulphate-free formula is worth trying; our guide to [sulphate-free and paraben-free shampoo](/blog/sulphate-free-and-paraben-free-explained) explains what those labels mean and what they don't.

### Moisturising ingredients

These are the ingredients that make a shampoo feel kinder on a dry scalp:

| Ingredient | What it does | Where you'll see it on the label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glycerin | Draws water into the skin and hair | Glycerin |
| Panthenol | Helps skin and hair hold on to moisture | Panthenol (provitamin B5) |
| Aloe vera | Light, soothing hydration | Aloe Barbadensis Leaf Juice |
| Argan oil | Softens and adds shine to the lengths | Argania Spinosa Kernel Oil |

You don't need all of them. One or two in a gentle formula is plenty.

### Ingredients and products to be wary of

No ingredient is automatically bad, but if your scalp is dry these are worth noticing:

- **Strong sulphates** such as sodium lauryl sulphate (SLS), which clean very effectively and can leave a dry scalp feeling stripped.
- **Heavy fragrance,** if your scalp is sensitive or easily irritated.
- **Clarifying shampoos used every wash.** Great for removing build-up now and then, but too harsh for everyday use on a dry scalp.
- **Daily dry shampoo.** Handy between washes, but it can build up on the scalp. Wash it out properly every few days.

## How to wash a dry scalp

The right shampoo used the wrong way can still leave your scalp dry. This routine gets the most from any gentle formula:

1. **Use lukewarm water.** Hot water strips oils from the skin.
2. **Wet your hair thoroughly** so the shampoo spreads easily and you need less of it.
3. **Use a small amount of shampoo** and massage it into the scalp with the pads of your fingers, never your nails.
4. **Rinse thoroughly,** so no shampoo or product is left sitting on the skin.
5. **Condition your lengths, not your roots,** and leave it on for the time the pack suggests.
6. **Dry gently.** Blot with a soft towel, then use a cool or medium setting if you blow-dry, holding the dryer away from your scalp.

<Callout type="tip" title="Shampoo the scalp, condition the ends">Your scalp is where sweat, oil and product build up, so that's where shampoo belongs. Your ends are the oldest, driest part of your hair, so that's where conditioner belongs. Keeping the two apart helps a dry scalp and dry ends at the same time.</Callout>

### How often should you wash a dry scalp?

Often a little less than you do now. Two or three gentle washes a week suits many people with a dry scalp, while curly, coily and afro hair can often go longer. Washing more often isn't harmful with a mild shampoo, but every wash removes some natural oil. ## Habits that make a dry scalp worse

Small changes here often make more difference than switching shampoo:

- **Very hot showers.** Turn the temperature down a notch, especially in winter.
- **Scratching or scrubbing with your nails.** It irritates the skin and can make flaking worse.
- **A hairdryer on full heat, close to the scalp.** Use a cooler setting and keep it moving.
- **Washing several times a day with a strong shampoo.** Once is enough, with a gentle formula.
- **Leaving styling products or dry shampoo on for days.** Rinse build-up out properly.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

Most dry scalps settle with a gentler shampoo, lukewarm water and a few habit changes. A pharmacist is a good first stop if you're not sure whether it's a dry scalp or dandruff, as they can recommend a suitable shampoo.

Following NHS guidance, see a GP if:

- you've used an anti-dandruff shampoo for a month and things haven't improved
- your scalp is very itchy, or it's red or swollen
- you have flaky, itchy patches on your face or other parts of your body

A GP can check your scalp for skin conditions that might be causing the flaking and suggest the right treatment.

The best shampoo for a dry scalp is gentle and moisturising, used with lukewarm water and a light touch. Keep conditioner on your lengths, wash a little less often and turn the heat down. ## Frequently asked questions

### What shampoo is best for a dry, itchy scalp?

A mild, moisturising shampoo used with lukewarm water, with ingredients such as glycerin, panthenol or aloe vera. If the itching lasts more than a few weeks, or your scalp is red, sore or swollen, speak to a pharmacist or GP.

### How do I know if it's a dry scalp or dandruff?

Dry scalp flakes are usually small and white, and the scalp feels tight. Dandruff flakes are usually larger and can look greasy or yellowish. A pharmacist can help you work out which you have.

### Is anti-dandruff shampoo good for a dry scalp?

Not usually. Anti-dandruff shampoos are made to treat dandruff, and they can feel harsh on a scalp that's simply dry. Choose a gentle, moisturising shampoo for a dry scalp, and save anti-dandruff shampoo for dandruff.

### Should I wash my hair less if I have a dry scalp?

Often, yes. Washing a little less often, with a gentle shampoo and lukewarm water, gives the scalp's natural oils time to build back up.

### Does conditioner help a dry scalp?

Conditioner is made for your lengths rather than your scalp. Keep it off the roots, where it can build up, and focus on a gentle shampoo and lukewarm water for the scalp itself.

### Is argan oil shampoo good for a dry scalp?

A gentle argan oil shampoo is a good everyday choice if your scalp and hair feel dry, because it cleans gently and leaves the hair soft. It isn't an anti-dandruff treatment, so if your flakes look like dandruff, use an anti-dandruff shampoo instead.

### Why is my scalp drier in winter?

Cold air outside and central heating inside both pull moisture from the skin, and hot showers make it worse. Lukewarm water, a gentle shampoo and fewer washes usually help.
