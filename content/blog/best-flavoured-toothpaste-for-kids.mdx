The best flavoured toothpaste for kids is one that contains the right amount of fluoride for their age and a flavour they genuinely enjoy, because a toothpaste your child likes is a toothpaste they'll use twice a day without a fight. Check the fluoride level (ppm) on the pack first, then let your child choose the taste.

That order matters. Flavour gets the brush into their mouth, but fluoride and good brushing do the protecting. This guide explains what to look for on the label, how the different flavours compare, and how to buy flavoured toothpaste without paying over the odds.

## Why flavour matters more than you might think

Children generally prefer sweeter tastes than adults do, and some find strong mint harsh. Some describe it as "spicy" or say it burns. When the toothpaste itself is the thing they're resisting, no amount of cajoling fixes the problem.

A flavour they like changes the dynamic:

- **It removes an objection.** If the taste is pleasant, the argument shifts from "I don't want to" to "which one tonight?"
- **It gives them ownership.** Choosing their own tube makes brushing feel like theirs, not something done to them.
- **It makes two minutes easier.** A nice taste is easier to live with for the full brushing time.

## What to check before buying flavoured toothpaste for kids

A fun flavour is the easy part. These are the checks that matter more.

### Fluoride content

Fluoride is the ingredient that helps protect teeth against decay, so it's the first thing to look for. The NHS guidance is:

- **Under 3:** a smear of toothpaste containing no less than 1,000ppm fluoride.
- **3 to 6:** a pea-sized amount of toothpaste containing more than 1,000ppm fluoride.
- **Adults:** toothpaste with 1,350 to 1,500ppm fluoride.

The ppm figure is printed on the pack, usually near the ingredients. If you can't find it, or you're unsure what suits your child, ask your dentist or pharmacist.

### Age guidance on the pack

Some toothpastes are aimed at particular ages. Read the directions on the tube, and remember that the NHS amounts above still apply whatever the flavour.

### Whitening claims

Plenty of flavoured toothpastes, including Nice Smile, are described as whitening. Whitening toothpaste works by helping remove surface stains; it doesn't change the natural colour of teeth. If you have questions about whether a particular toothpaste suits a young child, your dentist is the best person to ask.

### Values that matter to your family

If you want vegan or cruelty-free products, check the label or listing. Every Nice Smile toothpaste is described on Amazon as vegan, cruelty-free and enamel-safe.

### Tube size

Smaller tubes are easier for little hands and fit neatly into wash bags. Nice Smile comes in 60g tubes, which also makes it simple to keep a different flavour in each bathroom.

## How to use flavoured toothpaste safely

A toothpaste that tastes like sweets needs a bit more supervision than a minty one. The NHS advises discouraging children from eating or licking toothpaste from the tube, and a delicious flavour makes that more tempting.

- **Squeeze it yourself.** Put the right amount on the brush for your child's age, then put the tube away.
- **Store it out of reach.** A high shelf or a cupboard is safer than the edge of the sink.
- **Spit, don't rinse.** Encourage your child to spit out the excess but not rinse with water, so the fluoride keeps working.
- **Supervise until at least 7.** The NHS advises helping or watching children brush until they're at least 7 years old.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is flavoured toothpaste bad for children's teeth?

No, as long as it contains the right amount of fluoride for your child's age and they use the correct amount. Toothpaste flavours don't come from sugar the way sweets do; manufacturers typically use sweeteners instead. If you want to know exactly what's in a particular toothpaste, check the ingredients list on the pack.

### What age can children use flavoured toothpaste?

Children can use a flavoured toothpaste from their first tooth, provided it follows the NHS fluoride guidance: a smear with no less than 1,000ppm for under-3s, and a pea-sized amount with more than 1,000ppm for 3 to 6 year olds. Always read the directions on the pack, and ask your dentist if you're unsure.

### Do kids need a special children's toothpaste?

Not necessarily. What matters most is the fluoride level and the amount used, not whether the tube says "kids" on it. Some children simply prefer a flavoured toothpaste because it's milder than a strong mint.

### Can adults use flavoured toothpaste too?

Yes. Nice Smile is described as suitable for kids and adults, and some grown-ups prefer fruity flavours to mint. The NHS recommends adults use a toothpaste with 1,350 to 1,500ppm fluoride, so check the pack to make sure it suits you.

### Which Nice Smile flavour should I try first?

It depends on your child. Fruit fans might start with Watermelon Fresh or Feelin' Grape, while sweet tooths may prefer Yummy Gummy or Candy Clean. A three-flavour pack lets them try several before you commit to a bigger bundle.
