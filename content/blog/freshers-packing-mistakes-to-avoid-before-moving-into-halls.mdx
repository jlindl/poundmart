The biggest freshers packing mistakes aren't about forgetting your phone charger or spare bedding. They're about toiletries: the stuff you use every single day that's awkward and expensive to buy in dribs and drabs once term has already started. Get your toothpaste and shampoo sorted before you leave home, and you'll save yourself a scramble around unfamiliar shops in week one, when the queues are long and your student loan hasn't landed yet.

Most of this comes down to timing and quantity, not brand snobbery. Halls bathrooms are small, often shared, and nowhere near a big supermarket. A bit of planning before you pack the car sorts out problems that otherwise turn into a daily annoyance for the whole term.

## Freshers packing mistakes that cost you money

The classic mistake is packing exactly one of everything: one toothpaste, one shampoo, one conditioner. It looks tidy in the box, but it means a trip to buy more within the first fortnight, usually from whichever shop is nearest to halls rather than wherever is cheapest. Convenience stores near student accommodation are convenient for a reason, and that reason isn't low prices.

The second mistake is the opposite problem: turning up with a suitcase full of full-size bottles because you panicked and bought everything in sight. Full bathrooms shelves in halls are genuinely tiny, and if you're sharing with flatmates, your allotted space might be a single shelf or a shower caddy. Six different half-used bottles taking up that space isn't storage, it's clutter.

The third mistake is assuming you'll just buy things as you go. Term starts fast: lectures, societies, a new kitchen to learn, new people to meet. Nobody budgets time in week one for toiletry shopping, and it's exactly the kind of small cost that creeps up when you're not tracking it. Stocking up once, before you leave, with a multi-pack that will see you through several weeks, solves all three problems at once. ## Sharing a bathroom without the daily friction

Shared bathrooms in halls run on a simple rule: whatever is smallest and most clearly yours causes the least argument. A single 60g tube of toothpaste or a travel-sized shampoo bar is easier to keep track of, easier to label, and far less likely to go missing or get knocked into the shower tray than a full-size bottle balanced on a narrow shelf.

If you're short on space, a plastic-free shampoo and conditioner bar is genuinely practical rather than just a nice-to-have. It takes up less room than a bottle, it won't leak in transit if you head home for reading week, and it doesn't need a dedicated shelf slot. The same logic applies to buying in smaller individual packs for the bathroom while keeping the bulk of your stock in your room: open one tube or bottle at a time, and restock from your own supply rather than the shops. There are more ideas for keeping shared space workable in the piece on [plastic free bathroom swaps](/blog/plastic-free-bathroom-swaps).

Label anything that's genuinely yours if you're in a flat of six sharing one bathroom. It sounds obvious, but it's the single easiest way to avoid the low-level tension that builds up over a term when nobody's sure whose shampoo is whose.

## Freshers packing mistakes with teeth and hair, and what to do instead

Brushing routines often slip in the first few weeks of university, not because anyone's being careless, but because the structure of home life disappears. There's no parent reminding you, and a 10pm lecture prep session can easily eat into the time you'd normally spend brushing. The NHS guidance doesn't change just because you've moved out: brush twice a day for about two minutes, last thing at night and at one other time, and spit rather than rinse afterwards so the fluoride keeps working. Adults should use a toothpaste with 1,350 to 1,500ppm fluoride, so it's worth checking the pack rather than assuming. It's also easy to forget a toothbrush needs replacing every three months, not just when the bristles look obviously worn. If you've got questions about enamel wear or sensitivity that go beyond routine care, a dentist or pharmacist is the right person to ask, not a search engine at midnight.

Hair care has its own version of the same problem. How often you need to wash your hair depends on your hair type, and that doesn't change at university, but your routine might slip if your usual products run out and you can't easily get to a specific shop. Argan oil shampoo and conditioner are worth having in a shared bathroom because they're straightforward: they soften and add shine without needing a complicated routine, which matters when you're also trying to remember your timetable. If your hair is curly or afro, a leave-in, no-rinse conditioner is a sensible thing to pack because it works between washes, which suits a term where your usual wash day might get knocked off course by an early lecture or a late night. ## Frequently asked questions

### What toiletries do students actually forget to pack for halls?

Toothpaste and shampoo are the two most commonly underestimated items, because they run out faster than people expect once they're used daily by one person in a small bathroom. A spare toothbrush and a travel-sized conditioner are also easy to forget until the first one you packed runs out.

### Is it better to buy toiletries before moving in or wait until I arrive?

Buying before you move in is almost always easier. You'll have more choice, you can compare pack sizes properly, and you won't be relying on whatever a nearby shop happens to stock in week one, when you've got plenty else to organise.

### How much toothpaste or shampoo should I pack for a term?

There's no fixed number, since it depends on how often you brush and wash your hair, but a multi-pack rather than a single tube or bottle generally covers a term without needing a top-up trip partway through.

### Are shampoo bars a good idea for a shared bathroom?

Yes, for space reasons as much as anything else. A bar takes up less shelf space than a bottle, won't leak if you're travelling home for a weekend, and is easy to keep separate from a flatmate's things.

### Can I return toiletries if I buy the wrong flavour or scent?

Items bought through Amazon can be returned within 30 days through Amazon's standard returns process, so it's worth checking the listing details, including flavour or scent options, before you order a bundle.
