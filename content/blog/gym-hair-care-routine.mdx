A good hair care routine for the gym is simpler than you might think: tie your hair up loosely before you train, deal with sweat at the roots afterwards (often a rinse or a quick scalp wash rather than a full shampoo), and keep the lengths conditioned so regular washing doesn't dry them out. Pack a small kit in your gym bag and your hair can keep up with your training plan.

Whether you're lifting three times a week, running before work or swimming lengths at the local leisure centre, this guide covers what sweat actually does to your hair, the best gym hairstyles, when to wash and when to skip it, how to protect hair from chlorine, and exactly what to keep in your bag.

## Does sweat damage your hair?

Sweat is mostly water with some salt. The water isn't a problem, but when sweat dries, the salt stays behind. Left there regularly, it can make hair feel dry and rough and leave your scalp feeling itchy or tight.

Add in the scalp's natural oil, a tight hairband and a hat or headband trapping heat, and it's easy to see why post-workout hair can feel flat and greasy at the roots but dry at the ends.

The good news is that sweat doesn't mean you need to shampoo after every session. What matters is:

- **Not leaving a sweaty scalp for hours**, especially under a hat or tied up tightly.
- **Rinsing away salt** when you've sweated heavily.
- **Keeping the lengths conditioned**, so more frequent washing doesn't dry them out.

## Before your workout: set your hair up for success

A few minutes before you train can save you a lot of hassle afterwards.

### Choose a loose, low-stress style

Tight, high ponytails every day put constant tension on the same spot and can lead to breakage. Pulling hair back tightly and repeatedly can also cause a type of hair loss called traction alopecia. Good gym hairstyles include:

- **A low, loose ponytail or bun**, secured with a soft scrunchie or spiral tie
- **A loose plait**, which keeps long hair tangle-free during running or classes
- **A claw clip**, for mid-length hair on lower-impact days
- **A pineapple** (hair gathered loosely on top of the head) for curly hair
- **A protective style**, such as braids or twists, for afro and coily hair

Change the position of your ponytail or bun from session to session so the strain isn't always in the same place, and avoid elastic bands with metal joins that snag.

### Keep sweat off your face and hairline

A soft, absorbent headband catches sweat at your hairline before it runs into your eyes. Wash it after every session, along with any caps or sweatbands.

### Protect your ends

A little leave-in conditioner on the ends before you train can reduce friction from hair ties and tangling during high-movement workouts.

## After your workout: to wash or not to wash?

This is the big question, and the answer depends on how much you sweated and your hair type. Use this as a guide:

| Session | How your scalp feels | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light (yoga, walking, easy weights) | Dry or barely damp | Let it air, shake out your roots and restyle |
| Moderate (a gym session with some sweat) | Damp at the roots | Rinse with water, condition the lengths or use a leave-in |
| Heavy (running, HIIT, spin, a hot class) | Soaked | Wash the scalp with a small amount of shampoo, condition the lengths |
| Swimming | Chlorine or salt water | Rinse straight away, then shampoo and condition |

### The quick post-gym routine

When you do wash after a session, you don't need a full salon-style routine:

1. **Rinse thoroughly** with lukewarm water to remove sweat and salt.
2. **Shampoo the scalp only**, with a small amount, and let the suds run through the lengths.
3. **Condition the mid-lengths and ends.**
4. **Squeeze out water** with a microfibre towel rather than rubbing.
5. **Finish with a leave-in** to detangle and keep hair soft.
6. **Let your scalp dry** before tying hair up again. Tying damp hair up tightly for hours can leave your scalp feeling itchy.

### The rinse-only option

If you train most days, a full shampoo every time can leave hair dry, especially if it's curly, coily or coloured. On lighter days, try rinsing your hair thoroughly with water, then applying a leave-in conditioner to the lengths. It removes much of the sweat without stripping the hair.

## Swimming: protect your hair from chlorine

Pool water contains chlorine, which can leave hair dry and rough, and sea water leaves salt behind. To protect your hair:

- **Wet your hair with clean water before you swim.** Hair that's already full of tap water absorbs less pool water.
- **Wear a swim cap** if you swim regularly.
- **Rinse as soon as you get out**, using the poolside or changing room showers.
- **Shampoo and condition after swimming**, paying extra attention to the lengths.
- **Take care with blonde or bleached hair.** Minerals in pool water can give light hair a greenish tint over time, so thorough rinsing is especially worth it.

## Adapting your gym hair care routine to your hair type

### Curly and coily hair

Frequent shampooing can leave curls and coils dry. On most days, rinse with water, then refresh with leave-in conditioner and a little water. Protective styles and a pineapple keep curls out of the way while you train, and a satin-lined cap or scarf is handy for the journey home.

### Fine or oily hair

Fine hair can look flat after a sweaty session. A quick scalp-only wash usually sorts it, with conditioner on the ends only. Keep heavier products away from your roots.

### Long hair

Plait it for running and high-movement classes to stop tangles. Detangle gently afterwards with a wide-tooth comb, starting from the ends.

### Coloured hair

Colour-treated hair can be more fragile, so be gentle. Rinse chlorine out straight after swimming and don't skip conditioner.

## Look after your scalp

Your scalp is skin, and regular exercise asks a lot of it.

- **Let it dry.** Don't leave damp hair tied up tightly for hours after a session.
- **Wash hats, headbands and scrunchies regularly.**
- **Don't scratch.** If your scalp itches after training, a rinse usually helps.
- **Get persistent problems checked.** If you have ongoing itching, flaking or soreness, a pharmacist can recommend a suitable treatment, and your GP can help if it doesn't improve.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I wash my hair after every workout?

Not necessarily. After a light session, letting your hair air and restyling is often enough. After a sweaty workout, a rinse with water and some conditioner or leave-in usually does the job, with a scalp-only shampoo when you've sweated heavily or your roots feel greasy.

### Is it bad to let sweat dry in your hair?

Once in a while it's fine, but regularly letting sweat dry can leave salt behind that makes hair feel dry and your scalp feel itchy. A quick rinse with water after heavy sessions is a simple way to avoid it.

### Can I use a shampoo bar at the gym?

Yes, shampoo bars are ideal for the gym because they're compact, light and can't leak. Wet your hair, rub the bar between your hands or directly over your hair to make a lather, then rinse. Let the bar dry out between uses so it lasts longer.

### What is the best hairstyle for the gym?

A loose, low style that keeps hair off your face without pulling: a low ponytail or bun with a soft scrunchie, a loose plait, or a pineapple for curly hair. Avoid scraping hair into the same tight high ponytail every day, as repeated tension can cause breakage.

### How do I stop my hair getting greasy at the gym?

Wear a headband to keep sweat off your hairline, keep heavy styling products away from your roots, and wash your scalp after very sweaty sessions. Avoid touching your hair during your workout, as that transfers oil from your hands.
