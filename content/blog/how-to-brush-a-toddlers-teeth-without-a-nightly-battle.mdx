How to brush a toddler's teeth comes down to three things: twice a day, two minutes, and fluoride toothpaste at the right level for their age. Nobody expects a two-year-old to brush like a dentist. At this stage you're building a habit that will last, and you're doing most of the actual brushing yourself until your child has the hand control to do it well, usually around age seven.

The battles are normal. The squirming, the clamped lips, the sudden hatred of mint: most parents go through all of it. What makes a difference is a routine that's easy to repeat every single night, even when you're tired and the bath's running late.

## How to brush a toddler's teeth, step by step

1. **Pick a position you can hold.** Standing behind your toddler with their head tilted back against you gives you a clear view and stops them wriggling away. Some parents find it easier sitting on the edge of the bath with the child on their lap, facing away.
2. **Use the right amount of toothpaste.** A smear for under-threes, a pea-sized blob for three to six year-olds. More toothpaste doesn't clean better, it just gets swallowed.
3. **Brush in small circles**, covering the front, back and chewing surfaces of every tooth. Pay extra attention to the back teeth and the gumline, where plaque tends to build up unnoticed.
4. **Aim for two minutes**, twice a day: once in the morning and once last thing before bed. The bedtime brush matters most, because saliva flow drops overnight and sugars left on teeth have longer to do damage.
5. **Spit, don't rinse.** Rinsing with water washes away the fluoride that's just been doing its job. A quick spit into the sink is all that's needed.

If your toddler clamps their mouth shut, singing a short song while you brush, or counting teeth out loud, can buy you enough time to get round the whole mouth without turning it into a standoff.

## Getting the fluoride level right for their age

Fluoride is what actually protects the teeth, so the strength of the toothpaste matters more than the flavour. NHS guidance is specific by age:

- Under 3s: a smear of toothpaste with no less than 1,000ppm fluoride.
- Ages 3 to 6: a pea-sized amount with more than 1,000ppm fluoride.
- Adults and older children: toothpaste with 1,350 to 1,500ppm fluoride.

Always check the fluoride level printed on the side of the tube or the packaging before you buy, since it varies between products and isn't always obvious from the front of the pack. ## Frequently asked questions

### At what age should I start brushing my toddler's teeth?

As soon as the first tooth appears, usually around six months. Brush twice a day from that point using a smear of fluoride toothpaste with no less than 1,000ppm, building up to a pea-sized amount around age three.

### How do I get a toddler to open their mouth for brushing?

A consistent position, a short song or counting game, and letting them hold the brush first all help. Avoid turning it into a negotiation; a calm, matter-of-fact approach most nights works better than persuasion each time.

### Is flavoured toothpaste as effective as mint for toddlers?

Flavour doesn't affect how well toothpaste cleans or protects teeth, fluoride level does. A fruity or sweet flavour your toddler will actually tolerate is a better choice than a mint toothpaste they refuse to use.

### When should I take my toddler to see a dentist?

The NHS recommends children see a dentist regularly from the time their first tooth appears, and sooner if you notice anything unusual such as discolouration, pain or swelling. A dentist or your GP is the right person to ask about any concerns beyond everyday brushing.

### How long should a tube of toddler toothpaste last?

It depends on how much is used per brush and how often, but a 60g tube generally lasts several weeks with twice-daily smear or pea-sized amounts.
