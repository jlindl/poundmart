A complete travel toiletries checklist has four parts: daily basics (toothbrush, toothpaste, deodorant, shampoo and body wash), skin and sun care, a small first aid kit, and any medicines you rely on. The trick is packing them so they don't leak, don't take over your bag and don't get pulled out at airport security. Swapping liquids for solids where you can, and checking your airport's current liquid rules before you fly, removes most of the stress.

Below is the full checklist, how liquid rules work, which solid swaps are worth making and how to pack a wash bag that survives the journey.

## Liquid rules for hand luggage: check before you pack

"Liquids" at airport security means more than drinks. It covers gels, pastes, creams, lotions, aerosols and liquid-solid mixtures, so toothpaste, mascara, suncream and roll-on deodorant all count.

For years the standard rule at UK airports was containers of no more than 100ml each, packed in a single clear resealable bag of about 20cm by 20cm. Newer security scanners have allowed some airports to change their limits, and the rules have shifted more than once in recent years. They vary from airport to airport, and the airport you fly home from may do things differently.

So before every trip:

1. Check your departure airport's website for its current liquid rules.
2. Check the rules at your return airport as well.
3. If in doubt, pack for the stricter rules.

## Swap liquids for solids

The simplest way to stop worrying about liquid limits is to pack fewer liquids. Solid toiletries have come a long way, and several everyday products now have a good solid version:

- **Shampoo and conditioner bars** replace two bottles.
- **Bar soap** in a tin replaces body wash.
- **Solid deodorant** replaces a roll-on or aerosol.
- **Balm sticks** cover dry lips and skin.

Solid bars aren't usually treated as liquids at security, but because rules differ, check with your airport if you're unsure.

### Keeping a bar happy on the move

The one catch with bars is that they need to dry out between uses. Carry yours in a tin or a draining soap case, and leave the lid off overnight wherever you're staying so it doesn't go soft.

## Oral care on the go

Holidays are no reason to skip brushing. The NHS recommends brushing for about two minutes, twice a day, including last thing at night, and spitting rather than rinsing afterwards. Late nights and changed routines make it easy to forget, so keep your toothbrush somewhere you'll see it.

- **Toothpaste counts as a liquid** at security, so it goes in your liquids bag.
- **A compact tube saves space.** Check the size printed on the pack against your airport's limit.
- **A toothbrush cover** keeps your brush clean in your bag, but let the brush air-dry first.
- **Check the water.** In some destinations, local advice is to brush with bottled water, so look up the travel health advice for where you're going.

## Haircare away from home

Sun, sea, chlorine and hotel hairdryers can all leave hair feeling dry. A few simple habits help:

- **Rinse your hair after swimming** to wash out chlorine or salt water.
- **Cover up in strong sun** with a hat or a scarf.
- **Pack a leave-in conditioner** if your hair tends to feel dry or frizzy, decanted into a small bottle for hand luggage.
- **Keep heat styling to a minimum** and let hair air-dry when you can.

## Health, sun and first aid

A small first aid pouch saves hunting for a pharmacy somewhere you don't know.

- Plasters, blister plasters and antiseptic wipes
- Painkillers you know you can take
- Rehydration sachets
- Insect repellent and bite cream
- Suncream and after-sun
- Travel sickness remedies, if you need them
- Any prescription medicines in their original packaging, with a copy of your prescription

Some countries restrict certain medicines, so check the rules for your destination before you travel. Your GP or pharmacist can advise if you're unsure.

<Callout type="warning" title="Keep medicines in your hand luggage">
Hold bags can be delayed or lost, so keep any medicines you need in your hand luggage and bring a little extra in case your return is delayed. ## How to pack a leak-proof wash bag

1. **Choose a wash bag with a waterproof lining.** If something leaks, it leaks into the bag and not over your clothes.
2. **Tighten every lid** and add a square of cling film under the cap of anything runny.
3. **Squeeze the air out** of tubes and soft bottles before closing them.
4. **Pack liquids upright** where you can.
5. **Put your liquids bag at the top** of your hand luggage so it's quick to take out at security.
6. **Use refillable travel bottles** rather than buying new minis for every trip.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does toothpaste count as a liquid in hand luggage?

Yes. Airport security treats pastes, gels and creams as liquids, so toothpaste has to meet your airport's liquid rules and usually goes in your clear liquids bag. Limits vary between airports, so check the current guidance before you fly.

### Can I take shampoo bars in hand luggage?

Solid shampoo bars aren't usually treated as liquids, so they can often go in your hand luggage without using up your liquids allowance. Rules can differ between airports and countries, so check with your airport if you're unsure. Pack the bar in a tin to keep it dry and tidy.

### What toiletries should I pack for a week away?

For a week, pack a toothbrush and toothpaste, shampoo and conditioner (or a 2-in-1 bar), body wash or soap, deodorant, moisturiser, suncream if you're heading somewhere sunny, and a small first aid kit. Add any medicines you need, with a little extra in case of delays.

### How do I stop toiletries leaking in my suitcase?

Tighten all lids, add a layer of cling film under the caps of liquids, squeeze the air out of tubes and pack bottles upright in a waterproof wash bag. Swapping liquids for solid bars removes the risk altogether for some products.
