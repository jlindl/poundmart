## Cruelty free toothpaste UK: what the label actually means

'Cruelty-free' means the finished product and its ingredients haven't been tested on animals. 'Vegan' means there are no animal-derived ingredients in the formula at all, such as certain waxes or glycerine sourced from animal fat. The two labels often sit together, but they're not the same claim, so it's worth checking both if it matters to your family.

What these labels don't tell you is how much fluoride is in the tube, or how well it'll clean your child's teeth. That's a separate question, covered by the fluoride concentration printed on the pack rather than by the vegan or cruelty-free claims. If you want the full picture on reading that number, there's a straightforward guide on [how to read fluoride levels on toothpaste](/blog/how-to-read-fluoride-levels-on-toothpaste), and a more general explainer on [how to check if toothpaste is vegan before you buy](/blog/how-to-check-if-toothpaste-is-vegan-before-you-buy).

As a quick reminder, NHS guidance is that everyone should brush twice a day for about two minutes, last thing at night and on one other occasion, then spit rather than rinse. Children under 3 need only a smear of toothpaste with no less than 1,000ppm fluoride, children aged 3 to 6 need a pea-sized amount with more than 1,000ppm, and adults should use a toothpaste with 1,350 to 1,500ppm. Always check the ppm figure on the box itself, since it isn't the same for every product.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is cruelty free toothpaste actually different from ordinary toothpaste?

Not in how it cleans teeth. The difference is in testing and ingredients, not in fluoride strength or technique. A cruelty-free, vegan toothpaste still needs to be used twice a day, in the right amount for the age of the person using it, to do its job properly.

### How do I know what fluoride strength my child needs?

Check the ppm figure printed on the box. NHS guidance is a smear with no less than 1,000ppm for children under 3, a pea-sized amount with more than 1,000ppm for ages 3 to 6, and 1,350 to 1,500ppm for adults. If you're unsure, a pharmacist or dentist can confirm what's right for your child.

### Will whitening toothpaste make my child's teeth whiter than they naturally are?

No. Whitening toothpaste removes surface staining from things like food and drink, but it doesn't change the natural colour of the enamel underneath. For a fuller answer, see [does whitening toothpaste work](/blog/does-whitening-toothpaste-work).

### Is it safe for an adult and a toddler to share the same tube?

The formula is suitable for both kids and adults, but the amount used should differ by age, following NHS guidance on smear versus pea-sized amounts. If the fluoride strength printed on the pack matches what's recommended for the youngest person using it, sharing a tube is generally fine.

### Does buying a bigger pack actually save money?

Generally yes: bigger bundles cost less per tube than buying single tubes repeatedly, which adds up across a household getting through toothpaste every few weeks.
