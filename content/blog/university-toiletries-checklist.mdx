A good university toiletries checklist covers six things: a shower caddy with shampoo, conditioner and body wash; oral care (toothbrush, toothpaste and floss); skincare and deodorant; a small first aid kit; shared-bathroom extras like flip-flops and a dressing gown; and a few spares so you're not paying corner-shop prices in week three. Pack those and you're set for halls, whether your bathroom is en-suite or shared down the corridor.

Below you'll find the full checklist in one table, what's worth buying before you go, and practical ways to make your toiletries budget last the whole term.

## Shower and haircare essentials

If your bathroom is shared, a **shower caddy** is the most useful thing you'll buy. It carries everything to the shower and back in one trip and keeps your things separate from everyone else's. Choose one with drainage holes so water doesn't pool at the bottom.

**Flip-flops** or sliders are the other must for shared showers. They're easy to wipe down and far nicer than bare feet on a floor used by a whole corridor.

### Keep your haircare simple

Halls are not the place for a 12-step routine. A shampoo and conditioner that suit your hair type cover most people. If your hair tends to feel dry, look for a conditioner with a bit more nourishment. Not sure how often you should be washing? ## Oral care: easy to forget, pricey to neglect

Toothpaste is the item most likely to be left behind in the bathroom at home, so put it on your list twice.

The NHS recommends brushing for about two minutes, twice a day: last thing at night and at one other time. Spit out the toothpaste afterwards rather than rinsing with water, so the fluoride keeps working. Adults should use a toothpaste containing 1,350 to 1,500ppm fluoride, so check the pack when you buy.

A few extras make student life easier:

- **A spare toothbrush.** The NHS recommends replacing yours every three months, which is roughly once a term. Our guide on [when to replace your toothbrush](/blog/when-to-replace-your-toothbrush) explains the signs to look for.
- **Floss or interdental brushes** for cleaning between your teeth.
- **A travel cover** for trips home, but let your brush air-dry in your room the rest of the time.

## Health and first aid basics

Freshers' week is famous for one thing apart from the parties: freshers' flu, the colds that do the rounds when lots of new people meet at once. A small kit means you won't be hunting for a pharmacy when you feel rough.

- Plasters and antiseptic wipes
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen (read the label and never exceed the stated dose)
- Cold and flu remedies, throat lozenges and tissues
- A digital thermometer
- Rehydration sachets
- Any regular prescription medicines, plus a note of what you take

**Register with a GP near your university** as soon as you can after you arrive. The NHS advises students to do this so you can get care quickly during term. For minor illnesses, a local pharmacist is a great first stop for advice.

## Shared bathroom and flat extras

Halls and shared houses vary a lot. Some supply toilet roll and cleaning products; many don't. Check with your accommodation before you go, then pack:

- **Hand wash and a hand towel** for the sink in your room, if you have one.
- **A small bin** for your room.
- **A dressing gown** for the walk to shared showers.
- **A microfibre cloth** to give the sink a quick wipe after you've used it.
- **An over-door hook** for towels and your dressing gown (check your hall's rules before sticking adhesive hooks to walls).

<Callout type="tip" title="Label your caddy">
In shared bathrooms, identical bottles have a way of going missing. A strip of washi tape or a sticker with your initials on each bottle (and on the caddy itself) saves an awkward conversation in the kitchen.

## How to buy toiletries on a student budget

Your student loan has to cover a lot, and toiletries are a cost that comes back every few weeks. A few habits keep it under control.

Buying a term's worth of basics before you leave home means you won't be paying convenience-store prices near campus. Multipacks and bundles work well here because you buy once and forget about it until the holidays.

## Frequently asked questions

### What toiletries do I need for university?

The essentials are shampoo, conditioner, body wash, a toothbrush and toothpaste, deodorant, face wash, moisturiser, a razor or grooming kit, period products if you need them and a small first aid kit. For shared bathrooms, add a shower caddy, flip-flops and a dressing gown. Pack a few spares so you don't run out in the first busy weeks.

### Should I buy toiletries before I go to uni or when I get there?

Buying before you go usually makes the first weeks easier, as freshers' week is busy and shops near campus can be pricey. Bring enough to cover at least the first few weeks, including spares of the things you use every day. Bulky shared items like toilet roll can be bought with flatmates once you arrive.

### Do I need a shower caddy for university halls?

If your bathroom is shared, a shower caddy is one of the most useful things you can pack. It lets you carry everything to and from the shower in one trip and keeps your products separate from everyone else's. Even with an en-suite, it helps keep a small shower tidy.

### How often should students replace their toothbrush?

The NHS recommends replacing your toothbrush every three months, or sooner if the bristles are worn. For most students that's about once a term. Packing a spare means you can swap it on time without a special trip to the shops.
