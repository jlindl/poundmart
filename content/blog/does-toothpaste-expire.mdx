Does toothpaste expire? Yes, it does. Many tubes carry a date, usually printed on the crimped end of the tube or on the box, and the manufacturer only stands behind the formula, including how well the fluoride works, up to that point. Past the date, toothpaste isn't usually dangerous, but it may be less effective, and the texture and flavour can change. Stored well and unopened, though, toothpaste keeps for a long time, which is good news if you like to buy in bulk.

This guide shows you where to find the date, how to decode the symbols, what actually happens to old toothpaste and how to store spares so a multipack saves you money rather than ending up in the bin.

## Does toothpaste expire? The quick answer

Toothpaste is made to stay stable for a good while, but not forever. Over time, ingredients can separate, the paste can dry out and the active ingredients may not work as well as they did when the tube was made. That's why manufacturers test how long their formulas stay stable, and why you'll usually find a date or a symbol telling you how long to keep it.

The simple rule: **use toothpaste before the date on the tube or box.** If you find an old tube at the back of the cupboard that's past its date, replace it. It's an inexpensive product, and fluoride is doing too important a job to take chances with.

## Where to find the date on a toothpaste tube

In the UK, everyday toothpaste is treated as a cosmetic product, so it follows cosmetics labelling rules. Products that last 30 months or less must show a date of minimum durability. Longer-lasting products can show a "period after opening" symbol instead. Here's what you might see:

| What's printed | Where to look | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "EXP" followed by a month and year | Crimped end of the tube, or the box | Use by the end of that month |
| "Best before end" and a date | Crimped end or box | Best used by the end of that month |
| An hourglass symbol with a date | Box or tube | Another way of showing a best before date |
| An open jar symbol with a number and "M", such as 12M | Box or tube | Use within that many months of opening |
| A string of letters and numbers, often starting with "L" or "Lot" | Crimped end | A batch code for traceability, not a date |

If you can't find a date at all, check the box before you recycle it. Some brands only print it on the outer packaging, so it's worth copying the date onto the tube with a marker pen. If there's only an open jar symbol, write the month you opened it instead.

## What happens when toothpaste expires?

An expired tube won't suddenly turn harmful at midnight on the date, but a few things can happen as it ages:

- **Fluoride may not work as well.** The manufacturer only guarantees the formula up to the date, so you can't be sure you're getting the full decay protection after it.
- **The texture changes.** Old toothpaste can separate into liquid and paste, dry out, harden near the nozzle or feel gritty.
- **The flavour fades.** That fresh watermelon or grape taste may be noticeably weaker.
- **It may look or smell wrong.** Discolouration, an odd smell or any sign of mould means it's time to bin it straight away.

Because fluoride is the ingredient doing most of the work against tooth decay (we explain how in [what fluoride does for teeth](/blog/fluoride-toothpaste-explained)), an out-of-date tube is a false economy. Replace it.

## How long does an opened tube last?

Once you open a tube, the printed date still applies, and most households finish a tube well before it's reached. How fast you get through one depends on how much you use and how many people share it. A family brushing twice a day will empty a tube much faster than one person using a pea-sized amount. We do the maths in [how long a tube of toothpaste lasts](/blog/how-long-does-a-tube-of-toothpaste-last).

If a tube has been open for a long time, perhaps a spare in a holiday wash bag, give it a quick check before use: look at the date, the texture and the smell.

## How to store toothpaste so it lasts

Good storage keeps your toothpaste fresh right up to its date:

1. **Keep the cap on.** An open nozzle lets paste dry out and collect bathroom grime.
2. **Store it somewhere cool and dry.** A cupboard is better than a sunny windowsill or a spot next to a radiator.
3. **Avoid extreme heat.** Don't leave spare tubes in a hot car or conservatory for weeks at a time.
4. **Keep the nozzle clean.** Try not to press your toothbrush against the opening, and wipe away any build-up.
5. **Store spares in their boxes** if they came in one, so you keep the date and details.
6. **Use the oldest first.** Put new tubes at the back of the cupboard and bring older ones forward.

## What else in your bathroom has a shelf life?

While you're checking toothpaste dates, it's worth a quick sweep of the rest of the cupboard:

- **Toothbrushes** don't have a printed date, but the NHS recommends replacing yours every three months, or sooner if the bristles splay.
- **Mouthwash** usually carries a date on the bottle, just like toothpaste.
- **Shampoo, conditioner and other haircare** often show the open jar symbol, so note roughly when you opened each bottle. If you keep spares, the same first in, first out rule applies to anything in our [haircare collection](/collections/haircare).
- **Travel minis and hotel freebies** tend to hide at the back of drawers for years. Check them before your next trip.

A five-minute tidy every few months means nothing past its best ends up in anyone's wash bag.

## The bottom line

Toothpaste does expire, and the date on the tube or box is the one to trust. Store tubes with the cap on in a cool, dry cupboard, use the oldest first and bin anything past its date or that looks, smells or feels wrong. Get those habits right and stocking up becomes a smart way to save, rather than a cupboard full of forgotten tubes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to use expired toothpaste?

Using toothpaste that's slightly past its date isn't usually harmful, but the manufacturer no longer guarantees how well it works, including the fluoride protection. The texture and flavour may also have changed. It's best to replace it, and to bin it straight away if it looks, smells or feels unusual.

### Where is the expiry date on a toothpaste tube?

Look at the crimped end of the tube first, where the date is often stamped alongside a batch code. If it's not there, check the box. You might see "EXP", "Best before end", an hourglass symbol with a date, or an open jar symbol showing how many months to use it within after opening.

### Does toothpaste go off in the heat?

Heat can speed up changes in texture and consistency, so store toothpaste somewhere cool and dry rather than on a sunny windowsill, next to a radiator or in a hot car. A cupboard is ideal for spare tubes.

### Is it worth buying toothpaste in bulk if it expires?

Yes, as long as your household will use every tube before its date. Unopened toothpaste keeps well when stored properly, and bigger packs usually bring the price per tube down. Check the dates when your order arrives and use the oldest tubes first.

### What's the difference between the batch code and the expiry date?

The batch code is a string of letters and numbers the manufacturer uses to trace when and where a product was made. It isn't a date. The expiry or best before date is usually labelled clearly with "EXP", "Best before end" or an hourglass symbol.
