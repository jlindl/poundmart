If you have curly or afro hair, knowing how to wash your hair properly means more than lathering up and rinsing. It means working in sections, cleansing the scalp without stripping the lengths, detangling while the hair is slippery with conditioner, and sealing in moisture before the hair dries. Get the order right and you can cut down on frizz, dry ends and the dreaded day-two flop.

This isn't about buying more products. It's about using the right steps, in the right order, so the hair you've got holds its shape for longer and feels less brittle by the end of the week.

## How to wash your hair properly: a step-by-step routine

Curly and afro hair is naturally drier than straight hair, because the natural oils from the scalp have a harder time travelling down a curved hair shaft. That's why a wash routine built for straight hair (quick shampoo, quick rinse, done) tends to leave curls rough and frizzy. Here's a routine that works with the hair's structure instead of against it.

1. **Start detangling dry, or with a leave-in or oil on the ends.** Finger-detangle or use a wide-tooth comb before you even get the hair wet. This reduces the amount of pulling you'll do later and cuts down on breakage.
2. **Section the hair.** Four to six sections, clipped up, make it far easier to get shampoo to the scalp and rinse it out properly, rather than tangling everything into one knot under the shower.
3. **Wet thoroughly.** Curly and afro hair can take longer to saturate than you'd expect. Give it a proper couple of minutes under warm (not hot) water before you add any product.
4. **Cleanse the scalp, not just the lengths.** Apply shampoo directly to the scalp, section by section, and massage with your fingertips. The lather that runs down the lengths as you rinse is usually enough to clean them. There's no need to scrub the ends.
5. **Rinse well, then condition.** Apply conditioner generously through the mid-lengths and ends, working it through with your fingers or a comb while the hair is still wet and slippery. This is often the easiest time to detangle, since the conditioner adds slip.
6. **Rinse conditioner with cool to lukewarm water.** This can help the hair cuticle lie flatter, which makes it look smoother and can reduce frizz as it dries.
7. **Apply a leave-in while the hair is still dripping wet.** This is the step people most often skip or get the timing wrong on. Hair absorbs product differently depending on how wet it is, and applying leave-in to soaking wet hair helps distribute it evenly rather than sitting on top.
8. **Seal with a cream or oil, then style.** This locks moisture in rather than letting it evaporate as the hair dries.

## Common mistakes that undo a good wash

- **Washing too often.** Over-washing strips natural oils that curly and afro hair needs to stay soft. Most people with curls do better stretching washes out across the week rather than washing daily. There's a longer answer on frequency in [how often should you wash your hair](/blog/how-often-should-you-wash-your-hair).
- **Using too much shampoo.** More product doesn't mean cleaner hair, it just means more rinsing, which can leave curls drier. If you're not sure how much to use, [how much shampoo should you use](/blog/how-much-shampoo-should-you-use) has a straight answer.
- **Rough towel-drying.** Rubbing curly or afro hair with a regular towel roughs up the cuticle and causes frizz. Blotting gently, or using an old cotton t-shirt, causes far less damage.
- **Skipping the leave-in step.** Shampoo and rinse-out conditioner alone often aren't enough for curls that dry out quickly. A leave-in conditioner applied to soaking wet hair makes a real difference to how the curls hold shape through the day; see [what does conditioner actually do for curly hair](/blog/what-does-conditioner-actually-do-for-curly-hair) for the detail.
- **Sleeping on cotton pillowcases without protection.** Hair that looked great on wash day can lose its shape overnight through friction. A satin or silk pillowcase, or a loose bonnet, helps curls survive to day two and beyond, covered in [how to sleep with curly hair](/blog/how-to-sleep-with-curly-hair).

## Dealing with frizz, dry ends and day-two curls

Frizz is usually a sign that the hair is losing moisture faster than it should, often because the cuticle isn't lying flat. Damp or humid weather makes this worse, since the hair absorbs moisture from the air unevenly. If frizz is a regular problem, especially in wetter months, it's worth reading [how to stop frizz in humid weather](/blog/how-to-stop-frizz-in-humid-weather) alongside your wash routine.

Dry ends are often the oldest part of the hair, having been through the most brushing, styling and weather. Trimming regularly helps, but so does making sure the ends get extra conditioner and a sealing oil every wash, rather than an even amount throughout. For a broader look at habits that quietly dry hair out over time, see [habits that make dry hair worse](/blog/habits-that-make-dry-hair-worse).

If your curls look great on wash day but have lost their shape by day two, the cause is often moisture loss overnight or product that didn't have enough hold to begin with. Combining a protective sleep routine with a leave-in conditioner that suits your curl pattern tends to close that gap. ## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I wash curly or afro hair?

There's no single answer for everyone, but many people with curly or afro hair find once or twice a week works better than daily washing, since it gives natural oils time to travel down the hair shaft. See [how often should you wash your hair](/blog/how-often-should-you-wash-your-hair) for a fuller breakdown by hair type.

### Should I shampoo before or after detangling?

Detangle before you wet the hair, or at least before you shampoo. Pulling a comb through dry, tangled curls that are also full of shampoo makes breakage more likely. Detangling first, then rinsing, then conditioning (when the hair has more slip), tends to cause less damage.

### Is it bad to wash curly hair with hot water?

Hot water can strip natural oils and leave curls feeling rougher and more prone to frizz. Warm water for cleansing, followed by a cooler rinse for the conditioner, is generally kinder to the hair cuticle.

### Do I need a separate leave-in conditioner if I already use a rinse-out one?

For a lot of curly and afro hair, yes. A rinse-out conditioner cleans and smooths, but a leave-in applied to wet hair holds moisture in through the day. There's more detail in [what is leave-in conditioner](/blog/what-is-leave-in-conditioner).

### Why does my hair feel great after washing but frizzy the next day?

This is usually down to moisture loss overnight, often from friction against a cotton pillowcase, or not enough sealing product after the wash. Protecting hair overnight and using a leave-in conditioner consistently both help it hold its shape for longer.
