Most kids hair care mistakes come down to timing and product choice rather than anything you're doing wrong as a parent. Brushing dry, wet hair that's been rubbed with a towel, using one shampoo for every head in the house, or packing full-size bottles for a two-night trip: these are all small habits that make hair harder to manage than it needs to be. Fix the habit and the daily battle usually gets a lot shorter.

This matters most at the two flashpoints every parent recognises: bedtime brushing and school mornings. Get those right and the rest of the routine tends to sort itself out.

## Kids hair care mistakes that make bedtime harder than it needs to be

The single biggest mistake is brushing dry hair, especially if it's curly, afro or prone to tangling. Dry hair has less slip, so the brush catches, pulls, and a two-minute job turns into a ten-minute standoff. Damp hair with some slip in it combs through far more easily.

A second mistake is skipping anything between the bath and the brush. Shampoo alone, even a gentle one, can leave hair feeling rougher once it dries, which is exactly when tangles form overnight. A leave-in conditioner applied straight after washing, while hair is still damp, coats each strand and makes the next brushing session noticeably calmer. [XHC Vegan No Rinse Conditioner](/shop/xhc-no-rinse-conditioner) is built for this: no rinsing, no extra step at the sink, just worked through damp hair before it's left to dry.

Third, a lot of parents reach for whatever brush is nearest, which is often too stiff or too narrow for a child's hair. A wide-tooth comb or a detangling brush, starting at the ends and working up towards the roots rather than dragging from the scalp down, does far less damage and hurts far less when it snags. ## Washing too often, or with the wrong product for the hair type

Washing a child's hair every single day is rarely necessary and can leave hair drier and harder to manage, particularly curly or afro hair, which naturally holds less moisture along the strand. Spacing washes out a little, and using a leave-in conditioner on the days in between to refresh and soften hair, often works better than washing more.

Another common mistake is using one adult shampoo on every head in the house regardless of hair type. A product chosen for an adult's fine, straight hair may not suit a child with thick curls, and vice versa. If you're not sure where to start for a child with curly or afro hair, [how to moisturise afro hair and keep it soft all week](/blog/how-to-moisturise-afro-hair) is a useful read alongside this one.

The third mistake is treating toothpaste and hair care as separate problems with separate solutions, when actually the same principle applies to both: strong mint flavours and stiff, scratchy textures put children off a routine before it's even started. Just as a child who hates mint toothpaste needs a fruity swap rather than a lecture about fluoride (see [toothpaste for kids who hate mint](/blog/toothpaste-for-kids-who-hate-mint)), a child who hates having their hair done usually needs an easier product, not more patience from you. A no-rinse conditioner that doesn't need standing still at the sink, or a mild bar that lathers quickly in the bath, removes a lot of the friction.

## Kids hair care mistakes made when packing for a trip

Half-term trips and family holidays bring their own version of the same mistakes. Packing full-size bottles 'just in case' means more weight in the case and more to spill, and most of it comes home unused. The other packing mistake is assuming the hotel will have something suitable. ## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I wash my child's hair?

There's no fixed rule, and it depends on hair type and activity level. Curly and afro hair generally needs washing less often than fine, straight hair, with a leave-in conditioner used in between to keep it soft. Watch how the hair actually behaves and adjust from there rather than sticking to a strict schedule.

### Is leave-in conditioner safe to use on young children's hair?

A leave-in conditioner designed for everyday use, like one made for curly, afro or damaged hair, is generally fine applied to damp hair after washing. If your child has a known skin sensitivity or scalp condition, check with a pharmacist or GP before introducing a new product.

### My child's hair tangles badly even straight after a bath. What's going wrong?

This usually means the hair is drying out before it's brushed, or it's being brushed from the roots down rather than the ends up. Brushing damp hair with a wide-tooth comb, starting at the ends, and using a leave-in conditioner straight after the bath usually makes a noticeable difference within a few washes.

### Are shampoo bars suitable for children?

Yes, a mild 2-in-1 bar works well for most children and has the advantage of being quick to use and easy to pack for trips. If your child has a specific scalp or skin condition, check with a pharmacist first, as you would with any new product.

### What's the quickest way to stop bedtime brushing being a battle?

Brush damp, not dry, work from the ends upwards, and use a leave-in conditioner to add slip. Those three changes together usually make more difference than any single brush or product on its own.
