The best shampoo for dry hair is a gentle, moisturising one: mild cleansers that won't strip your hair, plus conditioning ingredients such as argan oil, glycerin or panthenol. How you use it matters just as much. Shampoo your scalp rather than your lengths, follow with conditioner every time and wash a little less often.

Below, we'll cover why hair gets dry in the first place, the ingredients worth looking for (and a few to be wary of), a step-by-step wash routine, and the everyday habits that make the biggest difference.

## Why is my hair so dry?

Your scalp produces an oil called sebum, which naturally coats and protects your hair. Dry hair usually means that protection isn't reaching the lengths, or it's being stripped away faster than it can be replaced. Common causes include:

- **Heat styling.** Hairdryers, straighteners and curling tongs all draw moisture out of the hair.
- **Colouring and bleaching.** Chemical treatments lift the cuticle, the protective outer layer of each strand.
- **Sun, wind and cold.** Weather roughens the cuticle, and central heating dries the air indoors all winter.
- **Hard water.** Many parts of the UK have hard water, which can leave mineral deposits that make hair feel rough and dull.
- **Swimming.** Chlorinated pool water is drying, especially with regular lessons or training.
- **Over-washing or harsh cleansers.** Stripping the natural oils every day leaves lengths with less protection.
- **Hair texture.** Curly, coily and afro hair tends to be drier, because sebum travels less easily down a curl than a straight strand.

If your hair has become very dry, brittle or starts falling out suddenly, and you can't link it to any of the above, it's worth speaking to your GP. Changes in hair can occasionally be linked to things like thyroid problems or low iron.

## What to look for in a shampoo for dry hair

A shampoo's first job is to clean your scalp. For dry hair, you want one that does that job gently and leaves something useful behind.

### Gentle cleansing

Look for products described as gentle, mild or suitable for daily use. These tend to use softer cleansers that remove dirt and excess oil without leaving hair squeaky, which is often a sign it has been stripped. Very dry or curly hair may also suit a sulphate-free formula; our guide to [sulphate-free and paraben-free shampoo](/blog/sulphate-free-and-paraben-free-explained) explains what those labels mean and what they don't.

These are the ingredients that make a shampoo feel kinder on dry hair:

### Labels worth reading

"For dry or damaged hair" and "moisturising" are good signposts. "Paraben-free" tells you about the preservative system rather than how moisturising a product is, so treat it as a preference rather than a dry-hair feature.

## Ingredients that can make dry hair worse

No ingredient is automatically bad, and many people use these without any trouble. But if your hair is dry, they're worth noticing:

- **Strong sulphates** such as sodium lauryl sulphate (SLS). They clean very effectively, which can leave dry or curly hair feeling stripped.
- **Drying alcohols** such as alcohol denat. listed near the top. These are more common in styling sprays and gels than in shampoo. Fatty alcohols like cetyl or cetearyl alcohol are different: they soften hair and aren't drying.
- **Clarifying shampoos used every wash.** They're great for removing build-up once in a while, but too harsh for everyday use on dry hair.
- **Heavy fragrance** if your scalp is sensitive or easily irritated.

## How to wash dry hair properly

The right shampoo used the wrong way can still leave hair dry. This routine gets the most from any formula:

1. **Use lukewarm water.** Very hot water strips more oil from your hair and scalp.
2. **Wet your hair thoroughly** before any shampoo goes on, so it spreads evenly and you need less.
3. **Shampoo your scalp, not your lengths.** Massage a small amount into your roots with your fingertips. As you rinse, the lather runs through the lengths and cleans them gently enough.
4. **Wash once.** A second shampoo is only needed if you're removing a lot of product.
5. **Condition every time.** Apply conditioner from mid-lengths to ends and leave it for the time the pack suggests.
6. **Detangle with conditioner in,** using a wide-tooth comb and starting from the ends.
7. **Blot, don't rub.** Squeeze water out with a microfibre towel or an old cotton T-shirt rather than scrubbing with a rough towel.

<Callout type="tip" title="Shampoo the roots, condition the ends">This one rule fixes a lot of dry hair. Your scalp is where the oil and sweat build up, so that's where shampoo belongs. Your ends are the oldest, driest part of your hair, so that's where conditioner belongs.</Callout>

### How often should you wash dry hair?

Usually less often than you think. For dry hair, two or three washes a week is often plenty, while curly, coily and afro hair can often go a week or longer. Washing daily isn't harmful in itself if you use a gentle shampoo, but every wash removes some of the natural oil your lengths need. ## Habits that help dry hair beyond shampoo

Shampoo is one piece of the puzzle. These habits often make a bigger difference:

- **Condition every wash,** and use a richer mask or deep conditioner once a week if your hair is very dry.
- **Seal your ends** with a leave-in conditioner or a drop of oil. Our guide to [argan oil for hair](/blog/argan-oil-for-hair-benefits) shows how much to use for your hair type.
- **Turn the heat down.** Use a heat protectant, the lowest setting that works, and air-dry when you can.
- **Sleep on silk or satin,** or wrap your hair, to reduce friction on the pillow.
- **Get regular trims.** Split, dry ends can't be repaired, only removed.
- **Protect hair in the pool.** Wet it with tap water before you swim so it absorbs less chlorinated water, then rinse and condition afterwards.

## Dry scalp or dandruff?

Flakes don't always mean a dry scalp. A dry scalp tends to feel tight and itchy with small, white flakes. Dandruff flakes are often larger and can look slightly greasy or yellowish. Moisturising shampoo helps the first, but dandruff usually needs an anti-dandruff shampoo, and a pharmacist can recommend one. If your scalp is sore, very itchy or not improving, see your GP.

## The bottom line

A good shampoo for dry hair cleans gently, contains a few moisturising ingredients and is used mainly on the scalp. Combine it with conditioner every wash, less heat and fewer washes, and dry, rough hair usually becomes softer and more manageable over time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is sulphate-free shampoo better for dry hair?

It can be, especially for very dry, curly or colour-treated hair, because sulphate-free formulas tend to be gentler. That said, plenty of people with dry hair do well with a regular gentle shampoo. If your hair feels squeaky, rough or tangled straight after washing, a sulphate-free option is worth trying.

### Can shampoo alone fix dry hair?

Not usually. Shampoo's main job is cleaning, so even a moisturising formula only goes so far. Conditioner, less heat, fewer washes and regular trims make most of the difference, and the shampoo's role is to avoid undoing that good work.

### Why does my hair feel dry straight after washing?

The most common reasons are water that's too hot, too much shampoo on the lengths, a harsh cleanser or skipping conditioner. Hard water can also leave hair feeling rough. Try lukewarm water, shampooing only the scalp and conditioning every time before you change anything else.

### Should I use a 2-in-1 shampoo for dry hair?

A 2-in-1 is convenient for short hair, travel or the gym, but very dry or long hair usually benefits from a separate conditioner. Separate products let you put shampoo on your scalp and conditioner on your ends, which is exactly what dry hair needs.
