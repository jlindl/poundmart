The back to school toiletries that matter most are the ones that make mornings smoother: a toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste your child is happy to use, a hairbrush and hair ties, deodorant for older children, and a small toiletries kit for the PE bag. Add a spare toothbrush, a supply of their favourite toothpaste and a wash bag ready for school trips, and you're covered for the whole autumn term.

Whether your school went back in mid-August in Scotland or goes back in early September in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, this checklist covers home, school bag and PE kit, with NHS-based brushing tips and ideas for stocking up without overspending.

## The school-day brushing routine, the NHS way

Busy mornings are exactly when brushing gets rushed. The NHS guidance is simple:

- **Brush twice a day** for about two minutes: last thing at night and at one other time, such as after breakfast.
- **Use the right amount of toothpaste.** For children under 3, a smear of toothpaste with no less than 1,000ppm fluoride. For children aged 3 to 6, a pea-sized amount with more than 1,000ppm fluoride.
- **Spit, don't rinse.** Rinsing with water washes away the fluoride that helps protect teeth.
- **Help or supervise younger children.** The NHS suggests keeping an eye on brushing until around the age of 7.

Our guide to [how much toothpaste kids should use](/blog/how-much-toothpaste-should-kids-use) goes into more detail by age. Whatever toothpaste you choose, check the fluoride level on the pack against that guidance.

### Make brushing the easy part of the morning

Put brushing at the same point in the routine every day (after breakfast, before shoes) so it becomes automatic. A two-minute song or sand timer helps, and letting children choose their own flavour gives them a sense of ownership. ## Hair: calmer mornings, fewer tangles

Hair is often the slowest part of the school run. A few habits help:

- **Brush or detangle the night before** so mornings are just a quick tidy.
- **Set up a hair station** with brushes, ties and clips in one basket by the mirror.
- **Buy hair ties in bulk.** They vanish at a remarkable rate.
- **Try plaits and buns** on PE days, as they tend to stay neater for longer.

### For curly and afro hair

Curly and afro hair tends to be drier and more prone to tangling, so many families use a leave-in conditioner to keep it soft and manageable between wash days. ### Head lice checks

Head lice are very common in young children and nothing to be embarrassed about. The NHS describes a method called wet combing: wash the hair with ordinary shampoo, apply plenty of conditioner, then comb through from roots to ends with a fine-toothed detection comb. A check at the start of term catches them early, and a pharmacist can advise on treatment if you find any.

## PE bag and changing room essentials

Older primary and secondary pupils usually need a small toiletries kit for PE:

- **Deodorant.** Some schools prefer roll-ons or sticks to aerosols in changing rooms, so check the school's policy.
- **A small towel** if showers are used after PE.
- **A hairbrush and spare hair ties.**
- **A flannel or face wipes** for a quick freshen-up.
- **Period products** in a discreet pouch.

Keep everything in a zip pouch inside the PE bag so it travels with the kit.

<Callout type="tip" title="The Sunday bag check">
Make restocking part of the weekly routine: empty the PE bag, wash the kit, top up the toiletries pouch and check for spare hair ties. Five minutes on a Sunday evening saves a frantic search on Monday morning.
</Callout>

## School trips and residentials

Many schools run residential trips, from a night at an outdoor centre to a week away in Year 6. The kit list from school always comes first, but a typical wash bag includes:

- A toothbrush with a travel cover and a compact tube of toothpaste
- Shampoo and shower gel in small bottles
- A hairbrush, hair ties and a leave-in conditioner for curly hair
- Deodorant and any suncream the school allows
- A flannel and a small towel
- Any medicines, handed to staff in the way the school asks

Label every item, including the toothbrush. A compact 60g toothpaste tube fits easily in a small wash bag.

## Frequently asked questions

### What toiletries does my child need for school?

At home, your child needs a toothbrush, a fluoride toothpaste suitable for their age, a hairbrush and hair ties, and, for older children, deodorant and period products. For school, a small PE pouch with deodorant (if allowed), a hairbrush and spare ties is usually enough. Always check the school's own kit list, especially for trips.

### How much toothpaste should a child use?

The NHS recommends a smear of toothpaste with no less than 1,000ppm fluoride for children under 3, and a pea-sized amount with more than 1,000ppm fluoride for children aged 3 to 6. Children should spit out the toothpaste after brushing rather than rinsing. Check the fluoride level printed on the pack.

### Can children take deodorant to school?

Usually, yes, and older children often need it after PE, but policies differ. Some schools prefer roll-ons or sticks to aerosols in changing rooms, so check your school's rules before packing one.

### How often should I replace my child's toothbrush?

The NHS recommends replacing toothbrushes every three months, or sooner if the bristles become worn or splayed. Children can chew or press hard on their brushes, so check them regularly. Keeping a spare in the cupboard makes the swap easy.
