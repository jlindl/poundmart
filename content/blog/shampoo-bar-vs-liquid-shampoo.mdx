Shampoo bars and liquid shampoo both clean your hair well. The difference is in how they feel to use, how long they last and how much packaging you throw away. A bar suits you if you travel a lot, want a plastic-free bathroom or like a compact routine. A liquid shampoo suits you if you love a big lather, share with a household, or want a matching conditioner in the same range.

Here's how the two compare, side by side, and how to get the best from whichever you choose.

## The quick comparison

- **Lather:** liquid shampoo lathers faster and more. A bar lathers well once you work it between wet hands or across wet hair.
- **How long it lasts:** a bar that's kept dry between uses lasts a long time because it is concentrated. A liquid shampoo goes further if you only use a coin-sized amount.
- **Travel:** a bar wins. It's solid, so it doesn't count as a liquid in hand luggage and can't leak in a wash bag.
- **Packaging:** a bar usually comes with little or no plastic. A liquid shampoo comes in a plastic bottle.
- **Sharing:** liquid is easier when several people use the same shampoo every day.
- **Hair type:** both can work for any hair type. Very dry, curly or afro hair usually needs a conditioner afterwards either way.

### You love a rich lather

Liquid shampoo foams up instantly. If that feeling is part of why you enjoy washing your hair, a bottle is the better pick.

### Several people share the shower

A pump or flip-top bottle is easier to share than a bar, which wears down faster and goes soft if it sits in water.

## What's actually different inside

Liquid shampoo is mostly water, with cleansers, conditioning ingredients and fragrance mixed in, which is why it needs a bottle. A shampoo bar leaves most of the water out, so the same job is done by a smaller, concentrated solid. That's the main reason a bar can last a long time and travel so easily.

## Getting used to a shampoo bar

The first few washes with a bar can feel different. Lather builds more slowly, and if you're used to a big squeeze from a bottle you may use too much and leave hair feeling heavy. A few tips:

- **Wet your hair properly first.** Bars need plenty of water to lather.
- **Make the lather in your hands.** Then work it into your roots and let the suds run through your lengths.
- **Rinse for longer than you think.** Leftover product is the most common reason hair feels coated after a bar.
- **Give it a few washes** before deciding. If your hair still feels wrong after a week or two, a liquid shampoo may simply suit you better.

## How to make a shampoo bar last

1. **Keep it out of the water.** Use a draining soap dish or a hanging bag, not the edge of the bath.
2. **Let it dry fully between washes.** A wet bar softens and wears away quickly.
3. **Rub it on your hands or the roots, not all your lengths.** You need less than you think.
4. **Store it dry before travelling.** Pack it only once it is completely dry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are shampoo bars better than liquid shampoo?

Neither is better for everyone. Bars are better for travel and cutting plastic; liquid is better for a big lather and for sharing.

### Do shampoo bars last longer than bottles?

A bar kept dry between uses lasts a long time because it is concentrated. A bar left sitting in water wears down much faster.

### Can you take a shampoo bar in hand luggage?

Yes. A solid bar isn't a liquid, so it doesn't need to go in your liquids bag. Check your airline's rules before you fly.

### Do I still need conditioner with a shampoo bar?

If your hair is dry, curly or long, yes. A 2-in-1 bar includes conditioning, but very dry hair usually benefits from a separate conditioner too.
